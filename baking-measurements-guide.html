The Baking Measurements Guide

Baking is chemistry you can eat. Measuring accurately is the simplest way to get the same good result every time.

Weight beats volume

Volume measures such as cups change with how an ingredient is packed. Flour scooped straight from the bag can weigh 25% more than flour spooned in lightly. A digital scale removes that difference and makes recipes from anywhere in the world work in your kitchen.

Common cup weights

Ingredient1 US cup
All-purpose flourabout 125 g
Bread flourabout 120–130 g
Granulated sugarabout 200 g
Brown sugar, packedabout 220 g
Icing sugarabout 120 g
Butterabout 227 g
Rolled oatsabout 90 g
Cocoa powderabout 85 g
Milk or waterabout 240 g
Honeyabout 340 g

Figures vary slightly by brand. The Cups to Grams converter uses these values.

Spoons and small amounts

For baking powder, bicarbonate of soda, salt and spices, level measuring spoons are accurate enough. One teaspoon is 5 ml and one tablespoon 15 ml. Use the flat back of a knife to level the spoon; heaped spoons can double the amount and spoil the rise.

Eggs and butter

  • Eggs: a large egg is about 50 g without the shell. If a recipe is in grams, crack and weigh.
  • Butter: use the markings on the wrapper or weigh it. “Softened” means it dents when pressed but is not shiny or melting.

Liquids

Measure liquids in a clear jug at eye level, or weigh them: water and milk weigh about 1 g per ml. Sticky liquids such as honey and syrup are much easier to weigh directly into the bowl.

Converting a favourite recipe

Bake it once using your usual cup method, but weigh each ingredient as you go and write the grams on the recipe. Next time, use the weights. Your results will be consistent from then on.
