The Pan Size Guide

No pan the recipe asks for? You can usually use what you have, as long as you match the area and adjust the bake time.

Work with area, not diameter

A 23 cm round pan is not slightly bigger than a 20 cm one; it holds about 30% more batter. What matters is the surface area, because it decides how deep the batter sits and how fast it bakes.

Scaling the batter

Divide the area of your pan by the area in the recipe. That number is your multiplier for every ingredient. A 20 cm square instead of a 23 cm round, for example, is about 0.96, so you can use the recipe almost exactly as written.

Adjusting time and temperature

  • Shallower batter: check 5–10 minutes earlier.
  • Deeper batter: lower the oven by about 10°C and bake longer.
  • Dark metal pans: brown faster; consider lowering the temperature a little.
  • Glass or ceramic: heat more slowly but hold heat longer.

Fill level

Most cake pans should be filled about half to two-thirds full. Overfilled pans dome, crack or overflow; underfilled pans give thin, dry cakes.

Lining your pan

Grease the pan lightly, then line the base with baking paper cut to size. For tall cakes, line the sides too. A well-lined pan means the bake lifts out cleanly and the edges stay neat.
